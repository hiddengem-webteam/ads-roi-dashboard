## What stands out
- Both sales campaigns fell at the same time, and spend was flat: lookalike $1,513 to $1,465, retargeting $1,488 to $1,454 between the two halves. So a budget change didn't cause this.
- Retargeting fell the most. Over the whole 30 days it still looks twice as good as the lookalike, but in the last 15 days it was only slightly ahead (3.69x vs 3.20x).
- Both have been quiet since mid-September. No Meta-credited purchases for the lookalike since 14 Sep; retargeting has had them on only two days since 13 Sep (18 and 21 Sep).
- Actual direct bookings fell too, but less: PMS shows $39,498 from 24 bookings in the first half and $27,016 from 19 in the second, about 32% lower. Meta-credited revenue fell about 57%, from $23,927 to $10,317. So bookings did slow, but Meta's credit dropped faster than the bookings did.
- A full calendar doesn't explain it: next 30 days 43% booked with 103 nights open. Only The Bluff is nearly full, with 4 open nights.

## Why it would hold off on the reallocation
1. The case against the lookalike is weaker than it looks. A 4.00x is being compared with the campaign that re-targets the website visitors it sends; over the same recent window the two are within 0.7x of each other. The lookalike is also the only sales campaign bringing new people to the website; the giveaway sends people to the Instagram profile or Facebook page, so it won't replace that flow of website visitors.
2. Adding $50/day to retargeting right after its return fell by two-thirds goes against the doctrine: retargeting is limited by how many people are in its audience, not by budget. Fix order: more creative, then a bigger audience, then a budget trim. Cutting the lookalike would shrink that audience while adding money to reach it. Extra budget mostly means showing the same ads to the same people more often.
3. Three changes at once, each too big. The doctrine caps a budget change at 20% every 3–4 days, with one change per cycle. Pausing one campaign and raising two others in the same session would put retargeting back into Meta's learning phase and make it hard to tell which change helped.
4. There is a real case for more giveaway spend: next 90 days only 26% booked; the doctrine assumes guests book 45–90 days ahead, so top-of-funnel spend now is buying November and December stays. But the giveaway should be judged on cost per profile visit and new followers per dollar. Increase it in steps.

## What it would do instead
1. In Ads Manager this week: check retargeting frequency and CPMs, and how old the creative is in both campaigns. Both are named 08/21/2026, so if the ads haven't changed since launch they're about a month old. Because both campaigns dropped together, something they share is more likely the cause than a lookalike-specific problem: the creative, the website or checkout, the offer, or tracking. This is a guess to test, not a finding.
2. If retargeting frequency is high: refresh the creative and widen the audience (IG/FB engagers from the last 365 days and the GoHighLevel lead list). Don't raise the budget.
3. If you want to move money now: take 20% off the lookalike, put it into the giveaway, and leave retargeting where it is. Look again in 7 days. If the lookalike still has no credited purchases, trim it another step.

Next best action: check retargeting frequency before changing any budgets. That one number decides whether retargeting can use more money at all.
